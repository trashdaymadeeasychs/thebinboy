# Directory listings: where to list The Bin Boy, and exactly what to enter

Google checks whether your business details match across the web. Listings on
well-known directories that all say the same thing help you rank in local
results and give customers another way to find you.

**The one rule:** copy the details below exactly, everywhere. Same name, same
phone format, same website. Small differences, like "Bin Boy" vs. "The Bin
Boy" or a different phone number, weaken the signal.

## Tier 1: do these first (free, and the most impact)

These feed the maps and assistants most people use, beyond Google.

- [ ] **Google Business Profile.** See `docs/google-business-profile-checklist.md`.
- [ ] **Apple Business Connect** (`businessconnect.apple.com`). Controls how
      you appear in Apple Maps and Siri, which iPhone users search directly.
- [ ] **Bing Places for Business** (`bingplaces.com`). Feeds Bing and other
      services that use Bing's data. As far as I know, it can import your
      details from your Google profile, which saves time. Check that the
      imported details match the table above.
- [ ] **Facebook page:** you already have one. Make sure the name, phone,
      website, hours and service area match the table exactly.
- [ ] **Yelp for Business** (`biz.yelp.com`). Claiming the free listing is
      enough. Yelp will likely try to sell you ads; you don't need them for
      the listing to count.
- [ ] **Nextdoor business page** (`business.nextdoor.com`). Especially useful
      for a neighborhood service: neighbors recommend businesses to each other
      there, and the page is free as far as I know.

## Tier 2: home-services sites

These are where homeowners look for local services. Most have a free profile,
but charge for leads or ads.

- [ ] **BBB (Better Business Bureau)** (`bbb.org`). I believe businesses can
      have a free profile without paying for accreditation. Accreditation is
      optional and paid.
- [ ] **Angi** (formerly Angie's List; includes HomeAdvisor). A free profile
      is worth claiming. Paying for leads is a separate decision; read the
      terms carefully before agreeing to anything.
- [ ] **Thumbtack.** Customers request quotes there, and you pay per lead.
      Only worth it if the lead cost makes sense for $25–$80 jobs.

## Tier 3: local credibility

- [ ] **Chamber of Commerce membership:** for example the Charleston Metro
      Chamber of Commerce or the Greater Summerville / Dorchester County
      Chamber. These are paid, but a member listing gives you a trusted local
      link to your website plus networking with HOAs and property managers,
      who are good commercial leads. Check current prices first.
- [ ] **Local HOA and neighborhood newsletters or vendor lists**, where you
      already serve customers.

## Skip or be careful

- **"Submit to 100 directories" services.** These often create low-quality or
  duplicate listings that are hard to clean up later. Listing yourself on the
  sites above is better.
- **Duplicate or old listings.** If you find an existing listing for The Bin
  Boy with an old phone number or a different name, claim it and correct it,
  or ask the site to merge or remove it. Don't create a second one.
- **Paying just to be listed.** For the sites above, the basic listing should
  be free. If a site insists on payment just to appear, it's usually not worth
  it.
